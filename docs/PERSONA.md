# PERSONA.md — The Tensile World

The fictional world all Atlas data is drawn from. Both the live demo data and the frozen eval fixtures come from here, so keeping names and facts consistent matters — an inconsistency shows up as a wrong answer that looks like a retrieval bug.

**Scope note:** this document shapes the data layer only. It determines what documents exist, what metadata fields they carry, and what the golden questions can ask about. It does not touch the retrieval, agent, or safety architecture.

## The Four Threads

Tension is what generates multi-hop questions. Each thread spans more than one source.

### 1. The raise
A term sheet arrived from Fernpath in July. Aster expressed competing interest but moved slower. Monthly investor updates go back to February, each with different ARR and burn numbers.

*Why it matters technically:* six near-identical monthly updates with different figures is the cleanest possible reranker test. Retrieving "the investor update" is easy; retrieving *June's* is not.

### 2. The customer at risk
Northwind Logistics is up for renewal in September. Somewhere in a sales thread, SSO was committed to them — with a date.

### 3. The roadmap
Lives in Notion. Whether it actually contains that SSO work, and by when, is the open question.

*Threads 2 and 3 crossed are the flagship demo:* "Did we commit SSO to Northwind, and is it on the roadmap?" Neither Gmail nor Notion alone can answer that.

### 4. The senior hire
Two final candidates for Head of Engineering, with compensation under discussion and reference checks in flight.

*Why it matters technically:* candidate emails carry names, salary figures, and personal details. This is the honest justification for PII redaction — not a decorative feature.

The distinction that matters: a hand-written document that no question ever asks about is functionally noise. Don't spend exacting effort on content that only needs to look plausible.

### What difficulty actually comes from

An earlier version of this document targeted ~1,300 chunks on the assumption that a bigger index means harder retrieval. That reasoning was wrong and worth recording.

An AWS invoice is not a plausible candidate for *"what did we promise Northwind"* — dense retrieval discards it for free. Four hundred more invoices would grow the index and change no query's difficulty.

What makes retrieval hard is **distractor density in the neighbourhoods where questions live**: documents that could plausibly answer the question and don't. Current density:

| Neighbourhood | Documents |
|---|---|
| ARR and financials | 52 |
| Compensation | 27 |
| Renewals | 20 |
| SSO and identity | 18 |
| Roadmap | 9 |

Those numbers, not the chunk count, are what to grow if v0.1 scores too high.

## Deliberate Traps

Built in so that specific versions have something to prove:

| Trap | Tests |
|---|---|
| 6 monthly investor updates, near-identical structure | Reranking (v0.4) — can it find *June's* numbers? |
| Pitch deck v3 vs v4 | Reranking — near-duplicate discrimination |
| SSO fact split across Gmail and Notion | Multi-hop decomposition (v1.0) |
| Distinctive proper nouns (Fernpath, Northwind) | Hybrid retrieval (v0.3) — exact match vs paraphrase |
| Salary figures and candidate details | PII redaction (v0.5) |
| Vendor and billing noise | Retrieval precision — irrelevant documents that shouldn't surface |
| Facts that exist nowhere | Graceful degradation — the system must refuse, not invent |

---

## Rules When Writing Content

1. **Names and numbers stay consistent.** If June's ARR is $47k in the investor update, it's $47k everywhere.
2. **Dates are explicit.** Every document carries a real date; the timeline has to hold together.
3. **Don't make everything relevant.** Roughly 70% should be noise.
4. **Vary the phrasing.** If every document about the raise uses the word "Series A," you've accidentally made retrieval trivial.
5. **Leave gaps on purpose.** Some reasonable questions should have no answer in the corpus.
