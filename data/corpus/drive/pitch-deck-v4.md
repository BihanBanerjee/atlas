# Tensile — Series A

**Version 4 · 17 July 2026**

---

## 1 — The problem

Engineering teams find out their system can't handle load at the worst possible moment: in production, during an incident, with customers watching.

Existing load testing tools are used once, before a launch, by one person who knows how to drive them. Then the results go stale and nobody looks again.

---

## 2 — What we built

Tensile is continuous performance testing. Tests run in CI on every change, results are diffed against the last known good, and regressions are caught before merge rather than after deploy.

**Tensile Load** — load generation and scenario testing
**Tensile Watch** — production performance monitoring
**Distributed Tracing** — GA August 2026

## 4 — Why retention is the number

118% net revenue retention at our ACV, in a category where most companies fight to hold 105%.

Expansion comes from two places: seat growth as more of the engineering org runs tests, and module attach as customers move from Load into Watch. Module attach is doing more of the work than we expected, which tells us the second product was the right one.

## 5 — Go-to-market

Self-serve generates most new logos, enterprise generates most new revenue. Enterprise deals close at 6.2x the ACV of self-serve.

Trial-to-paid conversion 6.6%, up from 4.1% after an onboarding rebuild in April.

---

## 6 — The enterprise opportunity

*New in this version.*

Our largest accounts are asking for capabilities we don't yet have. That is a constraint today and the clearest growth vector we have.

| Signal | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Enterprise ACV | 6.2x self-serve |
| Deals stalled on enterprise gaps, H1 | 2 |
| ARR with unmet enterprise requirements | $190,000 (22% of current) |
| Requirements named | Federated identity, audit export, SOC 2, EU residency |

Audit log export ships August. SOC 2 observation begins October. Federated identity and role-based access are scheduled for Q4.

Every one of these is a known, scoped, six-to-eight week build. This is not a research problem — it is a capacity problem, and capacity is what this round buys.

---

## 7 — Market

Performance testing and observability tooling. We sit between the two and win when the buyer is an engineering leader who has been burned by a launch.

Primary competitors: Surge Labs, Percival. We lose most often to incumbents bundling performance into an existing observability contract, which is a pricing problem rather than a product one.
