# Competitive Analysis

**Owner:** Marcus Oyelaran · Reviewed quarterly

## Direct competitors

### Surge Labs
Closest competitor by product shape. Stronger brand, weaker CI integration. Priced roughly 20% below us at comparable seat counts.

Where we win: teams that want testing in the pipeline rather than as a separate exercise. Their CI story is a plugin, ours is the product.

Where we lose: procurement processes that weight brand recognition, and any deal where SOC 2 is a gate — they have one.

### Percival
Open-core. Free tier is genuinely capable, which makes them the default for teams evaluating on their own.

Where we win: once a team hits scale, self-hosting becomes a job nobody wants. Our conversions from Percival are consistently accounts that grew past their own tolerance for operating it.

Where we lose: engineering-led teams with time and strong opinions.

## Indirect

Bundled performance features inside large observability platforms. This is where we lose most often, and it isn't a product argument — it's a line-item argument. The buyer already has a contract and adding us means a new vendor.

Two of the three deals lost in Q1 were this.

## Notes from H1

Our win rate against Surge Labs improved after the onboarding rebuild. Their trial is worse than ours now.

Enterprise gaps are the emerging pattern. In both stalled deals this quarter, the blocker was a capability neither competitor has either — we're losing to the absence of a category-wide feature rather than to a competitor. That suggests the first to ship it takes those deals.
