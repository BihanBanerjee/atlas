# Hiring Retrospective — H1 2026

## What we did

| Role | Opened | Closed | Time to close |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customer Success Lead | Jan | Mar | 9 weeks |
| Senior Backend Engineer | Feb | May | 13 weeks |
| Head of Engineering | May | Open | 8 weeks so far |

Two hires from three searches. Headcount 11 to 13 over the half.

## What went wrong

**The backend search took thirteen weeks and should have taken six.** Two offers
declined on compensation before we accepted the problem was our band, not the
candidates. Once we moved the band we closed in three weeks.

The cost of that delay was roughly seven weeks of an engineer's output, against
an annual band increase of about $18,000 for that role. We optimised the small
number and paid the large one.

**We interviewed nine people for the backend role.** Two would have been enough
had the band been right.

## What went right

**Referrals outperformed everything.** Both hires came through the network. Of
nine backend candidates, the two we made offers to were both referrals.

**Being honest in interviews worked.** Telling candidates what is broken — that
Dana carries too much, that enterprise capabilities are missing, that there is
no career framework — has not lost us anyone we wanted. One candidate said it
was why they proceeded.

## What to change in H2

- [ ] Check the band against benchmarking before opening any role, not after two declines
- [ ] Ask for referrals explicitly at the point of opening a role
- [ ] Keep telling candidates the unflattering things

## Open question

Aisha Rahman withdrew at final stage citing the reporting line — Head of
Engineering reporting to the CTO rather than the CEO. She is not the first to
raise it. Worth deciding whether that structure is a considered choice or an
accident we have not revisited.
